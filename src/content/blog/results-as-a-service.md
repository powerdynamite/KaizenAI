There are two common ways to pay for business software work, and both measure the wrong thing.

**Time and materials** bills you for hours. The supplier is paid for effort, so a project that runs long costs you more and costs them nothing. **Per-user SaaS** bills you for seats. You pay for every person who logs in, every month, whether the tool is delivering value that month or not, and the price grows with your headcount rather than with the results.

Neither model pays for the thing you actually wanted: a problem solved.

That is why we sell **Results as a Service**.

## What Results as a Service means

Results as a Service (RaaS) is simple to describe:

1. **We agree the result first.** In writing, before work starts: what will be delivered, and how we will both know it works. "Project managers submit status reports in Kaizen PPM, and the portfolio dashboard updates without manual work" is a result. "40 days of consultancy" is not.
2. **You pay a fixed price for that result.** One price, agreed up front. If it takes us longer than we planned, that is our problem, not your invoice.
3. **If we miss the agreed result, we fix it at no extra cost.** The guarantee is what makes the fixed price mean something.
4. **After go-live, a monthly managed plan keeps it running.** Updates, fixes, admin help and small changes, month to month.
5. **Changes are quoted at a fixed price too.** New features are planned with you and priced before any work starts. No surprise invoices.

## Why now

Two things changed.

First, **AI-assisted engineering changed the economics of delivery.** Work that used to take months now takes weeks. Under hourly billing, that saving goes to nobody, or to the supplier. Under a fixed price, part of it shows up in the price you pay.

Second, **seat-based pricing is under pressure.** As AI agents and Copilot take on more of the routine work, the number of people logging into a tool is a poor measure of its value. Many organisations are questioning why they should keep paying more each year to rent access to their own data.

Our view: you should pay for what the work achieves, own what you paid for, and keep it in your own tenant.

## What it looks like in practice: Kaizen PPM

Kaizen PPM, our project and portfolio management product, is the clearest example.

Most PPM tools are SaaS: per user, per month, for as long as you use them, with your project data in the vendor's cloud. Kaizen PPM works the other way round:

- **It runs in your own Microsoft 365 and Dataverse environment.** Your data never leaves it, and your existing security applies.
- **You pay once to deploy it,** at a fixed price that includes migration from Project Online or Planner if you need it. No per-user fee from us, however many people use it.
- **Then a managed plan** covers updates, fixes and help.
- **New capabilities,** such as an AI status-report assistant, are quoted at a fixed price when you want them.

Your users still need Microsoft licences (Power Apps premium or Dynamics 365), bought from Microsoft. We are explicit about that before you commit.

## When fixed price is the wrong answer

Results as a Service works when a result can be described. It does not work when nobody yet knows what the result should be.

If the problem is still fuzzy ("we want to do something with AI", "our CRM is a mess"), forcing a fixed price too early just moves the risk into padding or arguments later. In that case we start with a small, fixed-price **discovery step**, such as an architecture and roadmap session or a readiness assessment. Its result is a clear plan and, if you want it, a fixed-price quote for the next stage.

## Five things to agree before any fixed-price project

Whoever you work with, agree these in writing before work starts:

1. **The result, in plain words.** What will people be able to do that they cannot do today?
2. **How it will be accepted.** The tests or scenarios that prove it works, and who signs them off.
3. **What is out of scope.** The list of things that are explicitly not included is as important as the list of things that are.
4. **What you provide.** Access, licences, data, and the people who need to be available, and by when.
5. **How changes are handled.** Each change priced and approved before it starts, never added to the bill afterwards.

If a supplier will not commit to these, the price is fixed in name only.
