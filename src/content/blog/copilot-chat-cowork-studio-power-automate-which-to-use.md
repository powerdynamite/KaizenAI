Microsoft offers four overlapping ways to get work done with AI and automation. Pick the wrong one and you pay too much, get unpredictable results, or build something nobody uses. **The short answer: choose based on how predictable the work is and who starts it.**

## The four options at a glance

| Tool | Best for | Who starts it | How predictable |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Copilot Chat** | Quick questions, summaries, drafts | A person, one question at a time | Varies; a person reviews every answer |
| **Copilot Cowork** | Multi-step knowledge work with a finished deliverable | A person describes an outcome, or an event prompts a proposed task | Flexible; the AI plans the steps |
| **Copilot Studio** | Custom agents for a specific audience or process | Users of that agent, in Teams, a website or Microsoft 365 | Guided; you design the topics, knowledge and actions |
| **Power Automate** | Fixed, rules-based processes | Triggers such as a new record, a schedule or an approval | Fully predictable; same steps every time |

## How to choose

**Ask: "Would two good employees do this task the same way every time?"**

- **Yes, exactly the same way.** Use **Power Automate**. Approvals, data syncs and notifications don't need AI judgement, and deterministic flows are cheaper and easier to audit.
- **Mostly, but it needs reading and judgement.** Use a **Copilot Studio agent**. You define the knowledge sources and actions, so behaviour stays within bounds. A good fit for an HR policy assistant or a customer-service triage agent.
- **No, it depends on the situation and spans several systems.** Use **Copilot Cowork**. Account briefings, budget reviews and comparing large document sets are where its planning ability earns its cost.
- **It's a one-off question.** Use **Copilot Chat**. It's quick and cheap.

## They work best together

Real solutions often combine them. A Power Automate flow detects a new high-value case in Dynamics 365. Cowork prepares a briefing pack from the CRM, email history and contracts. A person reviews it and decides the next step. Each tool does what it is good at, and a person stays accountable for the decision. That matches our [human-in-the-loop principle](/blog/ai-agents-human-in-the-loop/).

## Three mistakes we see

1. **Using an AI agent for a fixed process.** If the steps never change, a flow is cheaper and more reliable.
2. **Building a custom agent when Cowork plus a skill would do.** A well-written skill file is often faster to deliver than a full Copilot Studio project. See [Cowork plugins and skills](/blog/copilot-cowork-plugins-and-skills-explained/).
3. **Skipping measurement.** Track time saved and cost per task for every option, especially usage-billed ones like Cowork.
