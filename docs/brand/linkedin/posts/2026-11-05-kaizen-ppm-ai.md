Most AI in project tools produces confident fiction.

The schedule is real, but the risk register is half empty and status lives in a slide deck. Put a language model on top and you get a fluent status summary that doesn't match the numbers.

In Kaizen PPM, every AI feature has to pass one test:
Does a PM or PMO make a different, faster or better-evidenced decision because this exists?

So AI sits on a four-rung ladder, and stops before the last rung:

1️⃣ Capture: less typing. Drop a charter on the form and review suggested values.
2️⃣ Surface: plain-language answers. "Which projects are over budget and slipping?"
3️⃣ Interpret: AI explains, never sets. Health is calculated by rules; AI explains it and cites the records.
4️⃣ Decide: always a human. AI can propose options. A person chooses.

The rule we never break: AI does not set project health. A persuasive narrative must never overwrite what the numbers say.

Our first AI build is status report drafting, because it's the biggest recurring time cost in most PMOs and the data is already in the system.
